## Why

A season currently stops before a marked week and can do nothing with it. `season-calendar`
materializes entries and waits for a `SeasonContestFact` that nobody produces; `week-loop`
returns control with `contest-ahead` and then advances the marked week as an ordinary one;
`contest-engine` resolves one Contest but is never called with a real opponent and its
authoritative `energyAfter` is never installed. Training, form and money therefore never meet a
competitive result, and a season cannot complete.

Issue #45 joins those three owners into one settled encounter: a marked calendar entry gets a
concrete opponent, the real resolver produces the result, and the outcome, participant energy,
reward and season fact are applied exactly once before time continues.

## What Changes

- Add a `season-contest` capability that owns the encounter: the binding between one marked
  `SeasonCalendarEntry` and one head-to-head `Contest`, the opponent behind it, the settlement
  of its consequences and the reward it pays.
- Bind exactly one supported Contest to a `contest`-marked entry. A `series`-marked entry has no
  supported format: settling one SHALL be rejected rather than collapsed into a single Contest or
  completed with an invented fact. The dependency on a separate series-format change is named
  rather than absorbed here.
- Materialize the season's opponent field once, from content, through a named `encounter` RNG
  stream: every definition of a caller-supplied validated pool gets exactly one opponent whose
  Performers are generated by the existing core generator with namespaced identities. Opening a
  marked entry draws one member of that field; drawing the same member twice in a season means
  the same Collective with the same people, not a regenerated lookalike. Re-opening an entry
  returns the stored encounter and moves no stream.
- Give every marked entry an explicit two-state lifecycle inside season state: `pending` once the
  opponent exists, `settled` once the Contest has been resolved and every consequence applied.
  Settlement is one atomic transition; a second call returns the stored encounter and moves no
  RNG, energy, balance, fact or season counter.
- Install participant consequences exactly once. The collective's Performers receive the
  Contest's authoritative `energyAfter` by replacement, never by a second subtraction of
  `nominalEnergyCost`. No morale, form, stat or age effect is invented. Opponent Performers stay
  inside the encounter's result and never enter career state or the field, so a second meeting
  starts from the same materialized state rather than a drained one.
- Add a new content kind, `content/encounters/*.json`, declaring a stable id, an English label,
  the discipline, the opponent's origin and level, and an explicit money reward for each of
  `win`, `loss` and `draw`. Draw is a declared row with its own amount, because regulation draws
  are a permanent outcome of the accepted `contest-engine` contract.
- Pay the reward exactly once inside settlement, through one organization change, before the
  marked week is advanced. Settlement executes no activity, no recovery and no recurring
  engagement debit, and it does not move the week index or the consecutive-negative-week count.
- Record exactly one `SeasonContestFact` per marked entry inside the same settlement, so the
  final encounter of a season is already counted when the last calendar week produces
  `season-ended` and the season completes.
- Retain both new continuations — the `contest` and `encounter` streams — in `RunState`, so they
  survive the season boundary, while the field and the encounters are season-owned and reset when
  the next season starts.
- Prove the whole path headless in core: materialized field → calendar entry → drawn opponent →
  real `resolveContest` → installed energy, paid reward and recorded fact → continued
  advancement, with a non-shipped season-template fixture that declares zero `series` weeks.

Out of scope: series, Bo3, Bo5 and big-event formats; intervention windows; standings, divisions,
seeding, brackets, placement or relegation; opponent progression between seasons, opponent
transfers or a simulated opponent world; changing the contest resolver's probability, draw or
metric rules; changing the recurring expense, the negative-week count or the season goal kind;
act-one economy balancing (#52); audience-scaled event income (#23); run failure conditions
(#32); UI, commentator text and presentation strings; `sim-harness` scenarios; golden snapshots
and balance baselines.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `season-contest`: encounter identity and unit, the season opponent field and its deterministic
  materialization, the pending/settled lifecycle, exactly-once participant, reward and fact
  application, the week and season boundaries the settlement must not cross, and the explicit
  continuation state.

### Modified Capabilities

- `season-calendar`: season state additionally retains its opponent field and its encounters, and
  starting the next season resets both together with the other season-owned accumulators.

## Impact

PR-2 will add one core encounter module and its exports, extend `RunState` with the two stream
continuations, extend season state with its opponent field and encounters, add the encounter
content schema, one shipped encounter definition and its referential validation, and add focused
core tests plus non-shipped fixtures. It touches no week-loop rule, no contest arithmetic and no
season goal formula.

The ordering against the week is decided here: an encounter is settled between weeks, on the
marked entry that is current, before that marked week is advanced. The week loop already returns
control on the preceding week with `contest-ahead`, so the settlement fits the existing boundary
without a new stop reason. Because the fact is recorded before the final calendar week advances,
the last encounter of a season reaches the season result without a second pass.

The shipped `standard` template declares two `series` weeks, which this change cannot settle. A
shipped season therefore still cannot complete until a series-format change exists; that
dependency is named in `design.md` and the headless proof uses a non-shipped zero-series template
fixture instead of a fabricated series result.

No accepted ADR is contradicted. Core keeps `Performer`, `Collective`, `Contest`, `Season` and
`Encounter` vocabulary (ADR 0001); opponent selection and generation draw only from one injected
named stream and settlement consumes no randomness of its own (ADR 0002); the opponent's origin,
level and every reward amount are validated content (ADR 0003). PR-1 changes only
`openspec/changes/season-contest/**` (ADR 0009).

## Player Experience (MDA / SDT)

The intended experience is that a week on the calendar finally means something: a named opponent
arrives, the roster you trained plays them, and the result lands on your people and your bank
account before the next week opens. The meaningful decision is upstream and now has a visible
price — how much energy the collective carries into a marked week, since the contest reads
opening energy and returns a lower one that the following week must recover from. Consequences
become legible through three separate pieces of evidence: the Contest's own moment feed, the
per-performer energy the settlement installed, and one reward line against the balance.

Competence is supported because the causal chain from preparation to outcome to money is
auditable and reproducible; autonomy, because the player chooses the state the collective enters
the week in, though there is deliberately no in-contest intervention yet; relatedness, because
the result is attributed to named performers on both sides and because the season's opponents are
a fixed field — meeting the same opponent twice means the same people, which is what lets a
rivalry exist at all later, without this change inventing a table, a seeding or a bracket. A draw
is deliberately a real third result with its own declared payout, so the player is never told a
story the engine cannot produce.

A falsifiable failure signal: if a reward table makes the outcome of the contest economically
irrelevant next to weekly activity income, the marked week stops being a decision and becomes a
cutscene. The measurable form is an act-one run in which total contest reward is a negligible
share of income while the season goal still passes — that would mean #45 wired the mechanic and
#52 has no lever worth pulling.
